About

Car Payment Calculator is a free, no-signup web tool that estimates monthly auto loan payments. It runs entirely in your browser — there is no server, no account, no advertising, and no tracking.

The goal is a transparent calculator that shows the math: real-time updates as you type, credit-tier APR estimates, state-level vehicle tax handling, full amortization schedules, and side-by-side term comparisons (24 to 84 months). The tool also runs in reverse — enter a monthly budget and it returns the maximum vehicle price that fits.

This calculator is also commonly known as a car loan calculator or auto loan calculator. The math is identical across all three names; we use "Car Payment Calculator" because that's the search phrase most U.S. buyers type when trying to figure out what they can afford.

Who built this

Built and maintained by an independent developer. No corporate affiliation, no lender partnerships, no sponsored content (as of April 2026). If that ever changes, this page will be updated first.

Coverage

The amortization math works for any country and any currency. The ZIP-code-based vehicle tax lookup covers all 50 U.S. states plus the District of Columbia and Puerto Rico. Users in Canada, the U.K., or elsewhere can leave the ZIP field blank and enter their local tax rate manually — all other features still work.

Methodology

Loan math

The standard amortization formula used by U.S. banks and required for Truth in Lending Act disclosures:

M = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1)

Where M is the monthly payment, P is the principal (the financed amount, after down payment, trade-in, and applicable tax), r is the monthly interest rate (APR ÷ 12 ÷ 100), and n is the number of monthly payments. The reverse mode solves the same equation for P given M.

APR estimates by credit tier

The default APR shown when you select a credit tier (Excellent / Good / Fair / Poor) is a snapshot of national averages drawn from Bankrate Auto Loan Average Rate and the Experian State of the Automotive Finance Market report.

These are reviewed quarterly; last reviewed April 2026. Your actual rate from any specific lender will depend on your full credit profile, the vehicle, the loan term, and current market conditions, and may differ by 1–4 percentage points in either direction. Always shop multiple quotes before signing.

State vehicle tax rates

The ZIP-to-state tax routing covers all 50 states, the District of Columbia, and Puerto Rico via a two-tier lookup: 2-digit ZIP prefix maps to the dominant state for that range, with 3-digit overrides for boundary ZIPs that fall into a different state (Rhode Island within Massachusetts prefixes, Delaware within Pennsylvania, Mississippi within Tennessee, Hawaii within California, Alaska within Washington).

Each state's rule is a small data structure containing the base rate, the trade-in credit policy, and any applicable flat tax cap. Sources cross-referenced from each state's department of revenue or DMV publications, plus the Tax Foundation annual sales tax compendium. Reviewed quarterly.

Trade-in tax credit handling

Most states reduce the taxable purchase amount by the trade-in value. This calculator models the following exceptions accurately:

  • No trade-in credit: California, Hawaii, Maryland, Virginia, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico — sales tax applies to the full purchase price regardless of trade-in.
  • Partial credit (capped): Illinois caps the credited trade-in amount at $10,000.
  • Flat fee with cap: South Carolina charges a 5% Infrastructure Maintenance Fee capped at $500, with no trade-in deduction.

Known limitations

This calculator deliberately does not model:

  • County or city local sales tax (which can add 0.5–4% in major metros — NYC ≈ +4.9%, Chicago ≈ +4%, Los Angeles ≈ +2.5%, Seattle ≈ +3.6%)
  • Georgia's Title Ad Valorem Tax (TAVT) is approximated as a flat 6.6% sales tax; the real TAVT is based on the vehicle's fair market value and applies once at purchase
  • Delaware's 4.25% motor vehicle document fee (Delaware has no sales tax)
  • Lender-specific origination fees, dealer doc fees, gap insurance, or extended warranties
  • Lease scenarios (this tool is for purchase loans only; lease math depreciates the vehicle differently)
  • Military, nonprofit, disabled-veteran, or other special-status exemptions that some states offer
For users in major metros: the most accurate number comes from looking up your actual combined rate (search "[your city] vehicle sales tax") and entering it manually in the tax rate field. The state base rate shown by the ZIP lookup is a starting point, not a final figure.

Privacy

Your inputs never leave your browser. This calculator runs entirely client-side. When you type a vehicle price, ZIP, or interest rate, those values are processed by JavaScript inside your own browser tab. There is no backend server. There are no API calls related to your inputs.

What we don't collect

  • No email address — there is no signup
  • No name, phone number, or other personal identifier
  • No account or login system
  • No session tracking, no fingerprinting, no behavioral profiling
  • No record of your ZIP, vehicle price, credit tier, or any other input

What we don't store

  • No cookies are set by this site
  • No browser localStorage or sessionStorage is used
  • No analytics SDK is loaded (as of April 2026)

Third parties

We don't currently self-host the fonts but may switch in a future update to remove this dependency.

Future analytics

Disclaimer

This calculator provides estimates for educational and planning purposes only. It is not a binding loan quote, lender offer, or financial advice.

Not a substitute for a lender quote

The APR shown for each credit tier is a national average snapshot, not the rate your specific lender will offer. Real lender quotes depend on your full credit report, vehicle details (year, model, condition, mileage), loan-to-value ratio, debt-to-income ratio, employment history, and current market conditions. Two lenders pulling the same credit report on the same day can offer rates that differ by 2–4 percentage points.

Not a substitute for a tax professional

Vehicle sales tax varies by county, city, and special district in addition to the state rate shown here. Some states apply different rules for new versus used vehicles, in-state versus out-of-state buyers, leased versus purchased vehicles, or military, nonprofit, and disabled-veteran exemptions. We do not model these. For an exact tax figure, consult your dealer's title-and-tax estimate or your state's department of revenue.
